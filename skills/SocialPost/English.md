# English posts

## Register per platform

| Platform | Register |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Professional and human: first person, contractions, plain verbs. Expertise shown through specifics, not adjectives. |
| X | Claim first, compressed, dry. One idea. |
| Threads, Bluesky | Conversational, like talking to people you know. No brand voice. |
| Mastodon | Plain, community-minded, generous with credit and alt text. |
| Facebook | Personal and story-shaped on a profile; warm and concrete on a Page. |
| Instagram, TikTok | The search phrase first, then a short human caption. |

## Rules that carry over from the Email skill

- Active voice and plain verbs: use, help, start, show. Not utilize, facilitate, leverage.
- One hedge per claim at most.
- Specific thanks: "Thanks to Dana for rebuilding the export in a weekend", not "Thanks to my amazing team".
- US or UK spelling from the profile, never mixed.
- Em dashes: one per post at most; a period or comma almost always works. One exclamation mark is plenty.

## Hebrew speakers writing English (Heblish)

Israeli authors' English posts pick up Hebrew structures. The lint bans the worst (`Tells.md`); also watch for:

| Calque | Native |
|---|---|
| "I will be happy if you share" | "If this is useful, pass it on" or nothing |
| "We are happy to announce that we raised" | "We raised $8M to…" |
| "As we spoke at the conference" | "As we discussed at the conference" |
| "Waiting to hear from you" | A specific question |
| "Send me a message" as a bare imperative | "Message me if you'd like the template" |

Israeli directness reads blunter in English than in Hebrew. A bare imperative to a US or UK audience lands as an order; turn it into an offer or a question.

## Hashtags in English

- CamelCase multi-word tags (`#OpenSource`, `#DevOps`): screen readers read them as words.
- Specific beats broad: `#PostgreSQL` over `#Tech`.
- Counts per platform: `Platforms.md`.
