# LinkedIn

Limits and folds: `Platforms.md` rows `linkedin` and `linkedin-comment`. Everything below was checked 2026-10-12.

## What the feed rewards now

- **Expertise and specifics.** LinkedIn's stated direction since 2023 is "knowledge and advice": posts with real professional perspective travel beyond the author's network.
- **Not slop.** From May 2026 LinkedIn demotes generic AI posts, engagement bait, and recycled "thought leadership", and lets members flag posts as "Seems like AI slop". Flagged posts stay visible to connections but stop spreading. LinkedIn named the "it's not X, it's Y" formula as an example. AI-assisted posts with original ideas are explicitly fine.
- **Policy.** The spam policy names reaction polls, chain posts asking for likes or shares, excessive or repetitive comments, and agreements to like or reshare each other's posts ("pods"). Automated comments get hidden.
- **Dwell time** is an official ranking signal: time spent on a post once half of it is visible. That's why a hook that earns the "see more" click matters.

## Writing it

- **Hook inside the fold**: the first line must work alone, within about 140 characters on mobile and 210 on desktop, or 3 lines, whichever comes first. Blank lines count as lines, so don't open with three one-word lines.
- **Length**: 1,300 to 2,500 characters for insight, lesson, and opinion posts; 300 to 800 for announcements and job changes. Directional, from creator-tool data.
- **Voice**: first person, professional but human. One concrete detail per post (`Craft.md`).
- **No broetry**: mix one-line paragraphs with two-to-four-sentence ones. A run of single-sentence paragraphs is the classic LinkedIn tell.
- **Hashtags**: hashtag following and hashtag feeds were removed in 2024 to 2025, so tags are a weak topic signal at most. 0 to 3 specific tags on the last line, never inline, never a block.
- **Links**: LinkedIn denies a link penalty, but datasets keep showing lower reach for link posts, and moving the link to the first comment is no longer reliably better. Make the post useful without the click, put the link at the end, and tell the user the trade-off. A link preview can't be changed after posting.
- **Mentions**: only people the user named, 1 to 3, each with a reason in the sentence. Mentions are dropped when the audience isn't Public.
- **Editing**: text can be edited after posting; images, documents, polls, and link previews can't. Get those right first.
- **Language**: Hebrew has been a LinkedIn interface language since September 2024, and readers get a "See translation" button. Israeli professionals post in English to reach global readers and in Hebrew for local reach and warmth. Ask which audience matters for this post.

## Formats

| Format | Write |
|---|---|
| Text post | The default. Hook, one idea, the detail, a specific close. |
| Document (PDF carousel) | A title, a slide-by-slide plan with one idea per slide and 10 to 25 words each, and a caption that works as its own post. Document posts top engagement benchmarks in 2024 to 2026. |
| Poll | Question up to 140 characters, 2 to 4 options of 30 characters. Ask something people genuinely disagree on. Never a reaction poll ("❤️ for A"). Polls can't be edited. |
| Video | The caption is its own post with its own hook. "Attention-bait" videos were named in the May 2026 crackdown. |
| Comment | 1 to 4 sentences adding one specific point. Never "Great post!", never the same text twice (`Workflows/Reply.md`). |
| Job change | Use LinkedIn's "Celebrate an occasion" option. The news, what you'll do, a specific thank-you, a forward line. |

## Israeli LinkedIn

Israeli readers punish American-style hype harder than US readers do: "גאה ונרגש לבשר", stacked gratitude, humblebrag, and broetry read as "לינקדאיני". Lead with the fact. Since October 2023, posts about reserve duty, hostages, and fallen colleagues are part of the feed: write them plainly, never as a career lesson or a sales hook (`TemplatesLinkedIn.md`, "Back after a long absence").
