# English: writing rules

Read with `Anatomy.md` and `Tone.md` for any English email.

## Voice

- Active voice, first person: "I'll send it Friday", not "It will be sent".
- Contractions in Professional and below (I'll, we're, don't). Fewer in Formal.
- Plain verbs: use, help, start, send, about. Not utilize, facilitate, commence, provide, in regards to.
- One hedge per claim at most: "I think we should move the launch", not "I just wanted to maybe suggest that perhaps".
- Specific beats general, in thanks and in praise: "Thanks for catching the pricing error" over "Thanks for all your help".

## Asking

- A question softens without weakening: "Could you send the signed copy by Thursday?"
- Give the day and date, and why it matters: "by Thu 16 Oct, so I can file on Friday".
- Offer a default so silence still moves things: "If I don't hear back by Wednesday, I'll book Tuesday 10:00."

## Saying no and delivering bad news

Decision first, reason second, alternative third. One apology at most, for the specific thing. "Unfortunately" once, if at all.

## Mechanics

- **Em dashes**: one per email at most; heavy use is the most recognized AI tell. A period or comma almost always works.
- **Exclamation marks**: one at most.
- **Spelling**: US or UK from the profile, otherwise the recipient's (color or colour, organize or organise). Never mix.
- **Dates**: "Thursday 16 October" or "Thu 16 Oct" for international readers, "Thursday, October 16" for US readers. Never 10/16 or 16/10 alone.
- **Times**: "3:00 pm" or "15:00", with a zone across borders.
- **Technical terms, product names, UI labels**: exactly as the product writes them ("click Enable", "the Staging environment"), no quotes needed.

## Hebrew-English calques (Heblish)

When the user writes in Hebrew, or the text started as Hebrew, these calques slip into English. Native readers notice them at once. `Tells.md` lists them for the lint.

| Calque | Hebrew source | Native English |
|---|---|---|
| "I will be happy if you send…" | אשמח אם תשלח | "Could you send…?" |
| "Attached please find the file" | מצ"ב הקובץ | "I've attached the file." |
| "As we spoke" | כפי שדיברנו | "As we discussed" |
| "In continuation to our conversation" | בהמשך לשיחתנו | "Following up on our call" |
| "I'm turning to you regarding…" | אני פונה אליך בנוגע ל | "I'm writing about…" |
| "Waiting for your answer" | מחכה לתשובתך | "Could you let me know by [day]?" |
| "Tell me if it's OK" | תגיד לי אם זה בסדר | "Let me know if that works." |
| "According to what we agreed" | לפי מה שסיכמנו | "As agreed" |
| "Please confirm receiving" | נא לאשר קבלה | "Please confirm you've received it." |
| "I'll be glad for your feedback" | אשמח לפידבק | "I'd appreciate your feedback." |
| "To close the subject" | לסגור את הנושא | "To wrap this up" / "to finalize" |
| "We are in the stage of…" | אנחנו בשלב של | "We're currently…" |
| "The meeting is in Sunday" | הפגישה ביום ראשון | "on Sunday" |

Directness transfers too. Hebrew imperatives that are polite in Israel ("Send me the updated file") read as orders in English. Turn them into requests: "Could you send me the updated file?"
